All 144 operations

Grouped by what they help you do. Or answer a few questions to find one for your situation.

Break the pattern I’m in

  • InterruptStop the habit at an arbitrary point, never a convenient one; look at what was about to happen before you resume.

Change what it is

  • InvertName the hidden assumption, then build its opposite; stop once the opposite stands up on its own.
  • TranslateCarry it into the new medium without adapting it; keep every loss, and stop when the whole has crossed.
  • ExaggerateOne feature, too big; still itself.
  • SubstituteSwap the occupant by a stated rule; keep its place, role and links.
  • ResizeChange the size of the whole by one factor; keep every proportion.

Get it back to a workable state

  • Clear And RefillBlank what you have used least, keep its place, and refill it fresh.
  • RecallMake an absent thing again from memory alone, never checking the original

Give me a useful restriction

  • ConstrainBan one whole category of choices; work inside what's left.
  • SteerShape the options so a free choice lands where you intend; the moment the steering shows, it has failed.
  • Suspend The RulesMark an edge in and an edge out, and suspend the ordinary rules only between them; they return at the edge out.
  • Don't Repeat YetWhat you just did is off-limits, and comes back only after enough different moves.
  • FunnelOne way in, one at a time; what does not fit stays out.
  • RelaxKeep the ban; set a price for each break; pay it every time; never change it.
  • Allow A MarginSet a band; leave whatever falls inside it; correct only what falls outside.
  • Set A RatioFix one ratio; derive every measure from it; measure nothing by eye.

Give me another way in

  • ScoreWrite only what must hold, never the result; if two runs come out the same, rewrite.
  • PlantIntroduce it early and say nothing about it; leave it alone until a later event makes it matter.
  • RoamHold one thing; let the rest wander.
  • Feed In OdditiesOnce an hour put something through the intake that does not belong; stop after five unknowns handled without widening or closing.
  • Leave OpenKeep both accounts at full weight; choose neither before the stated point.

Give me more to work with

  • ExhaustRecord every instance, the dull ones too; stop only when none are left.
  • AccumulateKeep adding; never select.
  • SaturateFlood one channel past capacity — and keep flooding.

Help me bring it to a close

  • End While It WorksSet the end while it still works and keep to it; never wait for it to fail.
  • Close Back To The StartA part is finished only when everything it caused work for is finished; stop when the first thing you touched closes by that rule.
  • RatchetEvery round spends something that cannot return.

Help me decide what comes first

  • Split The OrderWrite the making order and the experience order separately; stop when neither can be read off the other.
  • SequenceDecide the order yourself; an inherited order (alphabetical, as it came) is a refusal. Stop when every position has a reason.
  • Prepare EverythingPrepare everything before starting; search for nothing after.
  • AnchorFix one point; place every other part by its relation to it, and to nothing else.
  • RankOrder by importance, no ties; show the order in size or place.

Help me name what matters

  • Say What It Isn'tSay only what it is not; the first positive term ends it.
  • Find The KeystoneTest each part by taking it out, at least in rehearsal; stop at the one whose removal brings the rest down.
  • GeneraliseState the pattern as a rule an instance could break; stop when you can name the case that would break it.
  • NameFind the exact word, not the near one; stop when you can say what the name leaves out.
  • ClassifySort until the sorting rule appears.

Help me see it from another angle

  • ForegroundWork only on the incidental; no return to the official subject until the time you set is up.
  • Work The GapFind the space between two things and work in it; stop before anything fills it.
  • Make StrangeWork only from what is in front of you, as if you had never met it; stop when memory creeps back in.
  • LocateChoose where it stands and be ready to defend the choice; nothing starts until "here" is decided.
  • ReframeMove only the edge and leave the content alone; stop when every change you see traces back to the edge.
  • RelocateMove it untouched and let the new surroundings do all the changing; any change to the thing itself voids it.
  • SampleBuild the new thing around the found piece.
  • PinSweep an edge across once; keep what it caught at each instant; never go back over a strip.
  • Shade AcrossLay the categories along one range; place everything by position, between labels as often as on them.
  • PretendAct under one stated premise that is not true, for a set time, without ever testing it

Help the parts agree

  • TriangulateCome at it three ways that share no source; keep only what all three agree on.
  • AlignGive independently made parts one shared reference and align them to it; stop when they read as one.
  • MergeCombine until no seam shows and neither part can be got back.

Help the parts work together

  • Write The RulesWrite rules a stranger could run without asking you anything; stop editing when nobody needs to ask.
  • Build On The MarksThe trace decides where the next act goes.
  • Run On LeftoversRun a second process only on what the first leaves behind, so the first can keep going; when the first stalls, you have found the rate.
  • InterlockEvery group shares a member with every other; stop when a decision in one has reached all without a plenary.
  • Watch For ClashesName the one property that must never break; let everyone change things at once; step in only where two changes would break it together.
  • PullMake nothing ahead; replace only what is taken; stop when nobody takes.

Let it respond to what happens

  • Feed BackRoute the output back into the system and leave the loop closed until the system has acted on it.
  • Set Up The Next StageSet the conditions for the next stage and step away; if it still needs you, change the conditions, not the stage.
  • Raise The CostEach use raises the cost of the next; stop at the first skip and count the uses.
  • AmendOne rule governs how the rules change.
  • ReopenUnsettle one decision; change it; let it set again.
  • BreedPass on the thing and its rule; each maker changes the rule a little; stop after five passes.
  • MorphChange one step at a time toward the chosen form; every step must work.

Let me find out what holds up

  • RehearseDo the whole real act where failure costs nothing; keep none of the output, and stop before it starts to count.
  • DiagnoseReason from the symptoms to the cause and leave the symptom untreated; stop only when the cause is named.
  • Find The ThresholdVary one amount until it changes kind; cross the point from both sides and mark exactly where it is.
  • CalibrateCheck the work against the stated intention at fixed points, never against today's taste; correct only the drift.
  • Work What StayedPush, release, keep what stayed bent.
  • ScoutSmall probe first; grow only if it holds.
  • Test In GroupsTest in overlapping groups until every pair has been split once; the culprit sits in every failing group and no passing one.
  • Cross-CheckFour overlapping checks, each part in a unique combination; change the thing; the failing pattern names the part; fix only that.
  • ExploitObey every word; defeat the point; then fix the words.
  • ScaffoldAdd a support only for the making; take it out before the end; the work stands or falls.
  • HalveCut in two; keep the half that holds it; cut again; stop at one.
  • Follow ThroughTake the position at its word; add nothing; follow it until it breaks.

Make it something I can try again

  • IterateRun the cycle again on its own output; don't stop at one.
  • Load And RestAlternate load and rest on a fixed schedule; never skip the rest because things are going well.
  • RitualiseFix the form and the schedule, stated well enough to teach; repeat it exactly, every time.
  • ReplicateCopy exactly; change nothing.
  • MimicMake it pass as something else.
  • MirrorSet the axis first; give every part its partner on the other side.
  • AlternateSwitch between two on a fixed pattern; neither is the end.

Move the decision or responsibility

  • DeferHand the decision over and obey the answer; no taking it back, even when you dislike it.
  • ExchangePass it between parties in both directions; stop when each side holds something that came from the other.
  • Hand OverGive away the authority itself; after the handover, no advice, check-ins or quiet corrections.
  • ExecuteContinue what another began and keep every decision they made; no starting over.
  • WeatherSet it up; let the material make the marks.
  • LeaseGrant it with an end date; it lapses unless renewed.
  • Seed And LeavePut in one thing that does not belong; walk away; never tend it.

Protect what still needs to work

  • Run It LongSustain it until the length itself changes what it is.
  • ArchiveExclude something; keep what outlives the rest and treat everything else as gone.
  • OccupyTake the position and stay, without explaining why; the first justification turns it into a request.
  • Build In LayersFirst layer works alone; each next layer improves it and is not required; stop when the next would go unnoticed.
  • StitchRecord one part at a time without pausing the rest; note what crosses from recorded to unrecorded; one pass, then assemble.
  • Check Before AddingBefore adding anything, lay out one order in which everything, the addition included, can finish; if none exists, refuse; the first refusal is the operation working.
  • Keep A Way HomeKeep one plain route that always finishes; range anywhere you can still get back to it.
  • HoldFix one thing; change all the rest.
  • Keep BackKeep one part untouched; work the rest; read the two together at the end.
  • ReserveKeep some capacity unused and in order; give it no purpose in advance.

Pull the parts apart

  • Cut ThroughCut once, along the plane that reveals most.
  • IsolateHold everything else constant and change one thing; a second change voids the result.
  • Dissolve And RebuildBreak it down until no part survives intact, then rebuild from the residue alone.
  • PartitionDraw the line and give each side a different rule; if both sides keep the same rule, the line is decoration.
  • Freeze The JointFix the one joint exactly; change everything either side freely; where a change needs the joint to move is the edge.
  • Leave One OutEach round, leave out a different part by chance; bring everything back at the end.

Push me beyond what I’d normally do

  • Let DriftLet them drift; don't correct, don't force.
  • DefectArgue the other side at full strength, never as parody; stop only when you have made its best case.
  • Favour The RareBan whatever leads the tally, double whatever trails; stop when nothing leads by more than two.
  • Leave No WinnerClose the ring so nothing is unbeaten; then work on the one you would have discarded first.

Put different things into contact

  • GraftJoin them so they cannot be taken apart; stop when pulling would break something rather than separate it.
  • JuxtaposePlace them side by side with no caption; stop before you explain the pairing.
  • Join Through A ThirdPut no force on the pair; work only on the third element, and stop when it holds them without you.
  • Read The InterferenceOverlay the two systems and read only the interference; blame neither.
  • EntangleSeparate, and useless alone.
  • Share And Keep ApartMany on one surface, each recoverable.
  • Lay OverLay each layer over the last; keep each whole; read the stack.
  • FoldFold one part onto another; press once; open; touch nothing.
  • CoupleTie the two; change one; let the other move only through the tie.
  • ContainPut one whole inside another; keep both edges; the inner one comes out whole.

Shake up the order it is in

  • ScrambleBreak the order and keep every part; stop when nothing is where it was and nothing is missing.
  • Drop Needless OrderFor each pair of steps ask whether the second needs the first’s output; cut every order it does not; stop when a shuffle gives the same result.

Show me what I’m missing

  • TraceFollow the changes back link by link, on evidence only; stop where the evidence runs out, not where the story would.
  • AttendWatch with no goal and collect nothing; the first note or insight you take ends it.
  • RevealRearrange what is already there and add nothing; once it is revealed, leave it uncovered.
  • Mark TimeOne mark per fixed interval; a missed interval stays missed, and no mark is ever made late.
  • ElicitSupply only the shape of the prompt, never the content; everything that appears must come from them.

Spread it across more people or parts

  • DelegateKeep only the frame and hand every decision inside it to the group; never override what comes back.
  • Back Each Other UpOne job each, one other’s job badly; pull a part and see what the rest cannot carry.
  • HedgeStart several, let the world pick.
  • DisperseCut into more pieces than needed so any two-thirds work; pull a third at random; if it breaks, spread it and try again.
  • CoverMake the fewest groups in which every pair meets at least once; stop when the last pair has met.

Take something away

  • StripRemove one thing at a time; stop when the next removal would break it.
  • DegradeReduce step by step until it stops working; the failure point is the finding.
  • SacrificeDestroy something you truly value, with no stand-in; if it cost you nothing, it was not this.
  • CompressFind the smallest version that still carries everything.
  • DepriveRemove the whole channel, not some of it; keep it gone until the other senses have reorganised.
  • Let FadeIt fades unless you come back to it.
  • ShedDrop what the surroundings already do; point at where it went.
  • Mark What WentTake it out; keep its place empty and marked; let nothing fill it.
  • Cut The BeatenDrop only what another beats on every count; keep the rest, with their trade-offs.

Take something out of view for now

  • Forget For NowSet aside what you know and don't use it, however loud it gets; take it back only when the exercise ends.
  • ConcealWithhold it where everyone can see it is withheld; the cover never comes off.
  • Hide Until DoneCover each part as it is made; uncover at the end; change nothing after.
  • DelaySeal it with a date; nobody looks before it.
