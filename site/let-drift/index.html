Let Drift

Let them drift; don't correct, don't force.

Formerly Desynchronise

✓ Reich: two identical tape loops at slightly different speeds.

Seen in Steve Reich, It's Gonna Rain, 1965

Try it on your work

With your hands

Take two things you always use or move together and separate them in time: one today, its partner tomorrow, for three cycles. What each one turned out to do alone is the finding.

In words

Answer today's questions tomorrow, in writing, deliberately late, five times. Note which questions dissolved in the gap and which grew teeth.

In the process

Take two processes that run in lockstep in your project and offset them by one interval for one cycle. The friction points are where the lockstep was doing hidden work.

How it has been used

Let things that run together drift apart: the soundtrack off the image, the caption off the photo, two habits uncoupled. Steve Reich let two identical tapes slip out of phase and made a career of what appeared. The move forbids re-aligning them when it gets uncomfortable.
